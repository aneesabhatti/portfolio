01 — E-COMMERCE · CHECKOUT

Priceoye checkout & payments

An end-to-end checkout for Priceoye's website and app, covering OTP verification, contact info, delivery address and payment. The flow supports six payment methods, partial advance payments and post-purchase add-ons, written in Roman Urdu so it reads like the customer speaks.

UX flows UI design Responsive web Mobile app Design system
MY ROLEUI/UX lead: flows, UI, design system
PLATFORMSResponsive web and mobile app
COMPANYPriceoye
LANGUAGERoman Urdu UX writing
THE PROBLEM

Paying in installments means bank fees, identity checks and card limits. When these show up halfway through a form, customers stop. Checkout also had to fit six payment methods, part-payments and add-ons without becoming a wall of options.

CONSTRAINTS

One flow for web and app. Many customers think in Urdu but read Latin script on their phones, so formal English labels added friction. Cash on delivery is still expected, and stock is sometimes limited.

WHAT I DECIDED

Open one payment method at a time, in place. Show fees and requirements before any form field. Write every hint in short Roman Urdu. Offer warranty, insurance and gift wrap as toggles that never block checkout.

The flow, step by step

SPOTLIGHT — PAYMENT KAISE KARNI HAI?

Installments on one screen, with no surprises at checkout.

Paying in installments involves bank fees, verification and card limits. The screen opens the selected method in place and shows each step in the order the customer needs it.

  1. 1
    Six methods, one line eachEvery option has a short Roman Urdu hint, and only the selected one expands.
  2. 2
    Fees before commitmentThe processing fee and a four-point checklist (bank fee, CNIC match, credit limit) come before any form fields.
  3. 3
    Time-boxed sessionA countdown ring shows how long the customer has left to finish paying.
  4. 4
    Verification in a warm bandCardholder CNIC and phone fields sit on a tinted band and explain that the bank will call.
  5. 5
    Live plan calculatorChoosing a bank and tenure updates the monthly amount straight away.
  6. 6
    Trust at the point of entryCard network marks and an encryption note sit right next to the card fields.

TRADE-OFF

Opening one method at a time means customers can't compare two methods' details side by side. I chose a short list they can scan over a comparison table, because most customers arrive already knowing how they want to pay.

WHAT I'D MEASURE NEXT

Drop-off per payment method, how many customers choose partial payment, and add-on take-up at the cart.

03 — FINTECH · BNPL

SheenPay

End-to-end UI for a Buy Now, Pay Later mobile app, plus a conversion-focused landing page.

WHAT I DESIGNED
  • Onboarding that leads with the core promise: shop now, pay in 4, interest-free.
  • A home screen with BNPL apply, quick actions, top brands and promo banners.
  • Instalment status with paid and remaining amounts, a progress bar and dated due/paid rows.
  • Product pages with an instalment plan picker shown as pie progress for each payment.
  • KYC forms for employment, education and social profiles.
  • A marketing landing page that reuses the same app components.
MY ROLEProduct design, brand identity, launch
PLATFORMSMobile app and landing page
PARTNERPriceoye
MODELPay in 4, interest-free
THE PROBLEM

Instalment plans feel risky when people can't see what they owe and when. The app had to make "pay in 4, interest-free" believable from the first screen.

WHAT I DECIDED

Lead onboarding with the core promise. Show every plan as paid and remaining amounts, a progress bar and dated rows, so debt is always readable at a glance.

CONSISTENCY

The landing page reuses the app's components, so the promise looks the same before and after someone downloads the app.

BRAND & SOCIAL

Identity and launch campaign

A tilted S mark built from instalment blocks, a violet-and-aqua palette, and Poppins throughout, carried into the launch teaser and the #HalalBNPL post.

07 — OPERATIONS · MOBILE

Priceoye Hero rider app

An internal app for Priceoye's delivery riders. It shows today's tasks at a glance, gives each order a card with a one-tap call to the customer, and tracks delivered, returned and cash-on-delivery history over any date range.

Mobile app Internal tools Dashboard Empty states Illustration Kit design
MY ROLEProduct design and illustration
USERSDelivery riders, on the road
COMPANYPriceoye
TYPEInternal operations tool
WHO IT'S FOR

Riders check the app between stops, often one-handed and on patchy data. Every screen answers one question fast: what's next, who do I call, how much cash do I hold.

WHAT I DECIDED

One card per order with a one-tap call chip and colour-coded status. Today's counts on top of the dashboard, lifetime COD and distance below.

BUILT FOR BAD SIGNAL

The offline state shows the brand mark in grey with one line telling the rider what to do, instead of a blank error.

ENTRY

A "Priceoye Hero" sub-brand and rider illustration give the internal app its own identity. Sign-in is one card with two fields.
